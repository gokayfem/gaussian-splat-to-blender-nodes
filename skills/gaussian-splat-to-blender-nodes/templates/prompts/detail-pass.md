First correct spatial arrangement, envelope, negative spaces and contact. More density or
randomness cannot repair the wrong silhouette. Preserve measured regular patterns where
they exist; “organic” does not mean randomly perturb everything.

For each important part, inspect three scales:

- **Macro:** shape, dimensions, support, alignment, population envelope and occlusion.
- **Meso:** constituent pieces, joints, openings, thickness, folds, branch hierarchy,
  seams, edge wear, deposits and attachment/contact. Choose mechanisms relevant to this scene.
- **Micro:** correctly scaled grain, pores, scratches, pits, directional fissures and
  roughness/transmission variation, only where supported by the reference.

Use geometry where a feature affects outline, shadow or parallax. Repeated structures need
meaningful families, bounded variation and coherent placement rather than identical stand-ins
or uniform noise. Match material response as well as average color; do not globally darken
a surface to hide wrong sheen or double-light appearance sampled from the scan.

Inspect one coherent lighting setup across all views. Use a clay pass to distinguish form
and lighting from material errors. Preserve display/exposure conventions while comparing.
Treat regional color statistics as diagnostics, not percentages of fidelity.
